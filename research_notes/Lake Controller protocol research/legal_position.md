### Inferences
- Because the EULA defines "Software" only as "the Company's Software" that you "download, install and use", the natural reading is that it covers the Lake Controller desktop application (the thing you download and install), and arguably firmware delivered as an update through that application. It does not, on its face, reach (a) the behaviour of an amplifier you own, or (b) the bytes an amplifier emits on your own Ethernet network. Whether a court would stretch "reverse engineer any part of the Software" to cover observing the network output of the Software is the central contractual question and is unsettled (see Q2); a lawyer should read the clause against the specific captures you take.
- Note that the restriction is on reverse engineering "the Software", not on "the protocol" or "the devices". If you capture traffic between your own PLM/D-Series amplifier and a Lake Controller instance, the strongest argument that you have not touched "the Software" is to capture only device-to-device or device-to-your-own-tool traffic, and to avoid decompiling or disassembling the Lake Controller binary at all.
- The governing-law clause ("laws of the jurisdiction where you are a resident") means a US developer would be judged under the law of their home state plus US federal law (copyright, DMCA, DTSA); an EU/UK developer would get the benefit of the Software Directive's non-waivable Art. 5(3)/6 rights. The exclusion of the UCC is unusual and mostly affects warranty/sale-of-goods arguments.
- The mirrored text contains the stray reference "MG-IP", indicating this EULA was carried forward from the MUSIC Group era with minimal edits; it is a generic all-brands EULA, not a Lake-specific one.

## Key Question 2: Does passive packet capture count as "reverse engineering the Software", and how does US (and EU/UK) law treat it?

### Takeaway
US law gives no single clean answer: contractual anti-reverse-engineering clauses have been enforced (Bowers, Davidson/bnetd) even where copyright fair use would otherwise permit the activity (Sega, Connectix); the DMCA §1201(f) interoperability exemption exists but failed on the facts in bnetd; and trade-secret law expressly says reverse engineering is not an "improper means". In the EU/UK, by contrast, observing/studying/testing and interoperability decompilation are statutory rights that licence terms cannot remove (Directive 2009/24/EC Arts. 5(3), 6, 8; SAS v WPL). Passive observation of an unencrypted protocol on your own network is the lowest-risk method under every regime, but a contract claim cannot be ruled out in the US.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Contractual anti-reverse-engineering clauses in US courts**
- Bowers v. Baystate Technologies, 320 F.3d 1317 (Fed. Cir. Jan. 29, 2003): Baystate argued the Copyright Act preempts the shrink-wrap licence's reverse-engineering prohibition; the court "held that the Copyright Act does not preempt or narrow the scope of the contract claim" and affirmed the breach-of-contract verdict; Judge Dyk dissented [snippet] — [Justia case page](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/320/1316/615597/); [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-federal-circuit/1202776.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowers_v._Baystate_Technologies,_Inc.)
- Davidson & Associates v. Jung, 422 F.3d 630 (8th Cir. 2005) (the bnetd case): the court held that "State breach-of-contract claims survive preemption when contract terms conflict neither with nor restrict federal copyright rights" and that the defendants' breach of the EULA/ToS anti-reverse-engineering terms was not preempted [snippet] — [Google Scholar opinion](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_case?case=5463280175310353497); [Berkeley Law PDF](https://www.law.berkeley.edu/archive/files/Davidson_v_Jung.pdf); [Casemine](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/59147400add7b04934399488); [Wikipedia: Bnetd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bnetd)

**(b) Copyright fair use for interoperability**
- Sega Enterprises v. Accolade, 977 F.2d 1510 (9th Cir. 1992): intermediate copying/disassembly to discover unprotected functional requirements for compatibility is fair use where no other means of access exists [snippet, general] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega_Enterprises_Ltd._v._Accolade,_Inc.)
- Sony Computer Entertainment v. Connectix, 203 F.3d 596 (9th Cir. **(c) DMCA §1201 and the interoperability exemption**
- 17 U.S.C. §1201(f) verbatim (from a GitHub mirror of the statute; verify against Cornell LII when reachable): "(1) Notwithstanding the provisions of subsection (a)(1)(A), a person who has lawfully obtained the right to use a copy of a computer program may circumvent a technological measure that effectively controls access to a particular portion of that program for the sole purpose of identifying and analyzing those elements of the program that are necessary to achieve interoperability of an independently created computer program with other programs, and that have not previously been readily available to the person engaging in the circumvention, to the extent any such acts of identification and analysis do not constitute infringement under this title. (2) Notwithstanding the provisions of subsections (a)(2) and (b), a person may develop and employ technological means to circumvent a technological measure, or to circumvent protection afforded by a technological measure, in order to enable the identification and analysis under paragraph (1), or for the purpose of enabling interoperability of an independently created computer program with other programs, if such means are necessary to achieve such interoperability, to the extent that doing so does not constitute infringement under this title. (3) The information acquired through the acts permitted under paragraph (1), and the means permitted under paragraph (2), may be made available to others if the person referred to in paragraph (1) or (2), as the case may be, provides such information or means solely for the purpose of enabling interoperability of an independently created computer program with other programs, and to the extent that doing so does not constitute infringement under this title or violate applicable law other than this section. (4) For purposes of this subsection, the term 'interoperability' means the ability of computer programs to exchange information, and of such programs mutually to use the information which has been exchanged." — [gingerbeardman/canoe-disassembly README (mirror)](https://github.com/gingerbeardman/canoe-disassembly); [christopher-conley/OpenVersus README (mirror)](https://github.com/christopher-conley/OpenVersus); official text at [Cornell LII 17 U.S.C. 1201](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1201) (blocked in this environment)
- In Davidson v. Jung the Eighth Circuit set out the four elements of the §1201(f) defence: "(1) they lawfully obtained the right to use a copy of a computer program; (2) the information gathered as a result of the reverse engineering was not previously readily available to the person engaging in the circumvention; (3) the sole purpose of the reverse engineering was to identify and analyze those elements of the program that were necessary to achieve interoperability of an independently created computer program with other programs; and (4) the alleged circumvention did not constitute infringement" [snippet quoting the opinion] — [Studicata](https://www.studicata.com/case-briefs/case/davidson-associates-v-jung)

**(d) Trade secret law**
- 18 U.S.C. §1839(6): "improper means" "(A) includes theft, bribery, misrepresentation, breach or inducement of a breach of a duty to maintain secrecy, or espionage through electronic or other means; and (B) does not include reverse engineering, independent derivation, or any other lawful means of acquisition" [snippet paraphrase closely tracking statutory text] — [US Code, 18 USC 1839 (blocked; snippet)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title18-section1839&num=0&edition=prelim); [Finnegan overview of DTSA](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/what-you-should-know-about-the-defend-trade-secrets-act.html); [Cole Schotz summary](https://www.coleschotz.com/defend-trade-secrets-act-2016/)

**(e) Network packet inspection specifically**
- Practitioner commentary flags that packet inspection is legally risky "unless (1) the network is configured to be accessible to the general public; (2) you have consent of all users whose packets are intercepted; or (3) you have consent of the network provider" [snippet] — [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2021/03/27/reverse-engineering-law-understand-restrictions-minimize-risks/). (Capturing on a network you own, between devices you own and software you run, satisfies the consent conditions; this is an inference, see below.)
- A US patent application on protocol reverse engineering notes that "reverse engineering from network traffic captures may be the only or best option" and describes obtaining captures "by interacting directly with a device that uses the protocol" [snippet] — [USPTO publication](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11777832)

**(f) EU Software Directive 2009/24/EC and UK equivalents**
- Art. 5(3) verbatim: "The person having a right to use a copy of a computer program shall be entitled, without the authorisation of the rightholder, to observe, study or test the functioning of the program in order to determine the ideas and principles which underlie any element of the program if he does so while performing any of the acts of loading, displaying, running, transmitting or storing the program which he is entitled to do." — [Pitscheider/inim-prime-native LEGAL.md (mirror quoting the Directive)](https://github.com/Pitscheider/inim-prime-native/blob/d841840dfd69ddf467ec19e96fd2347a5e4fccdb/LEGAL.md); official text at [EUR-Lex 2009/24](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2009/24/oj/eng) (blocked)
- Art. 6(1) verbatim: "The authorisation of the rightholder shall not be required where reproduction of the code and translation of its form within the meaning of points (a) and (b) of Article 4(1) are indispensable to obtain the information necessary to achieve the interoperability of an independently created computer program with other programs, provided that the following conditions are met: (a) those acts are performed by the licensee or by another person having a right to use a copy of a program, or on their behalf by a person authorised to do so; (b) the information necessary to achieve interoperability has not previously been readily available to the persons referred to in point (a); and (c) those acts are confined to the parts of the original program which are necessary in order to achieve interoperability." — same mirror as above
- Art. 8, second paragraph, verbatim: "Any contractual provisions contrary to Article 6 or to the exceptions provided for in Article 5(2) and (3) shall be null and void." — [FezVrasta/ha-unipolsai README (mirror)](https://github.com/FezVrasta/ha-unipolsai); [msjang/krigf26-font-eula-survey report (mirror)](https://github.com/msjang/krigf26-font-eula-survey); confirmed by search summary of [legislation.gov.uk retained EU law copy](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2009/24/article/6/2020-01-31/data.xht?view=snippet&wrap=true)
- CJEU C-406/10 SAS Institute v World Programming (2 May 2012): "Neither the functionality of a computer program nor the programming language and the format of data files in order to exploit its functions constitute a form of expression of that program and are not protected by copyright"; a licensee may "observe, study, or test the functioning of the program to determine the ideas and principles which underlie its elements on condition that that person does not infringe the exclusive rights of the copyright owner" [snippet quoting the ruling] — [Society for Computers & Law report](https://www.scl.org/2451-sas-institute-inc-v-world-programming-ltd-report-and-analysis/); [European Law Blog](https://www.europeanlawblog.eu/pub/c-40610-sas-institute-v-world-programming-ltd-wpl/release/1); [RPC: final chapter](https://www.rpclegal.com/thinking/ip/no-copyright-in-software-functionality-sas-v-wpl-the-final-chapter/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAS_Institute_Inc_v_World_Programming_Ltd)
- UK: CDPA 1988 ss. 50B (decompilation for interoperability) and 50BA (observing, studying, testing) "cannot be excluded by contract"; s.50B(3) lets a supplier defeat the decompilation right by making interoperability information "readily available" [snippet] — [Practical Law Q&A](https://uk.practicallaw.thomsonreuters.com/a-133-5153?transitionType=Default&contextData=%28sc.Default%29); [legislation.gov.uk s.50B](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/50B/data.html)
- UK 2025: IBM v LzLabs (High Court) held that Art. 5(3)/s.50BA "observation, study and testing" "does not extend to reverse engineering, copying, or adapting source code or object code where there is a reproduction of the expression of the copyright author's own intellectual creation", i.e., licence-breaching disassembly went beyond the exception [snippet] — [Jones Day summary](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/05/uk-court-rules-on-reverse-engineering-of-mainframe-software); [RPC Part 1](https://www.rpclegal.com/thinking/tech/reverse-engineering-of-ibm-mainframe-software-in-breach-of-software-licence-ibm-v-lzlabs-part-1/); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=67d85e3d-0d9f-4916-a551-8826a9dc67ee)

### Inferences
- Passive capture vs. "reverse engineering the Software": Every US case that enforced an anti-RE clause involved decompiling, disassembling or otherwise copying the licensor's program (Bowers: decompiling a CAD add-on; bnetd: emulating Battle.net after analysing the client and its traffic). Watching your own amplifier's packets with Wireshark copies none of the Lake Controller's code. A reasonable argument is therefore that (i) the protocol is part of the devices' and software's functional behaviour, which under Sega/Connectix and SAS v WPL is unprotected by copyright, and (ii) observing it is not "disassembling, decrypting, reverse compiling or reverse engineering" the Software in the sense the clause targets. The counter-argument is that "reverse engineer any part of the Software" is broad enough to cover black-box analysis of the Software's outputs. No case found decides this on a passive-capture-only record; treat it as a live contract risk, not a copyright or DMCA risk.
- DMCA: If the Lake protocol is unencrypted and has no authentication handshake or key exchange, there is nothing that "effectively controls access to a work" to circumvent, so §1201(a) should not be engaged at all and §1201(f) is a fallback rather than a necessity. If the protocol turns out to include a challenge/response, encryption, or signed firmware, §1201 analysis changes materially (bnetd turned on Blizzard's "secret handshake"/CD-key check); stop and get advice at that point.
- Trade secret: An unencrypted protocol broadcast on the owner's LAN, observable by any lawful purchaser, is unlikely to meet the "reasonable measures to keep secret" element, and §1839(6)(B) expressly excludes reverse engineering from improper means. The residual risk is the argument that the EULA created a "duty to maintain secrecy" — again pointing back to the contract question.
- Wiretap/ECPA: Capturing traffic on a network you own, between hardware you own and software you run, is intercepting your own communications with your own consent; this is a common-sense reading rather than a cited holding, and should be confirmed by counsel if any capture is done on a venue's or client's network.
- EU/UK contrast: An EU-resident developer would have a statutory, non-waivable right to observe/study/test (Art. 5(3)) and, if needed, decompile for interoperability (Art. 6), with Art. 8 voiding the EULA clause to that extent; the EULA's "laws of the jurisdiction where you are a resident" clause would import those protections for an EU/UK user. A US resident gets no such statutory override — hence the US analysis rests on Bowers/Davidson (contract enforceable) versus Sega/Connectix (fair use), which is why the contract clause matters more in the US.
- Practical mitigations that follow from the case law: use only your own devices and network; do not decompile, disassemble, unpack, or debug the Lake Controller binary or firmware images; document that the protocol information was "not previously readily available"; keep a clean-room record (captures, hypotheses, tests); make the app an "independently created computer program" with no Lake code, assets, strings, or UI graphics copied; and if Music Tribe offers a documented API or SDK, use it (in the UK, availability of interoperability information defeats the decompilation right).

## Key Question 3: Distribution risk (App Store, trademark, Music Tribe's litigation posture, third-party app precedent)

### Takeaway
Distribution raises risk in three distinct ways: (1) the EULA's "commercially exploit ... or make the Software available to any third party" and reverse-engineering clauses become far more visible and give a motive to sue; (2) Apple Guideline 5.2.1 and 4.1(c) forbid using another company's brand or product name in your app name/icon without approval, so "Lake" cannot be the app's name, though descriptive compatibility statements ("works with Lake-enabled devices") are the industry norm; (3) Music Tribe/Empower Tribe litigates aggressively over hardware clones and defamation but has, for over a decade, tolerated and even hosted community discussion of third-party X32/X-Air/Midas control apps (Mixing Station, Patrick-Gilles Maillot's X32 tools), and has formally authorised at least one independent protocol document (WING). The 2025 Klon v. Empower Tribe case is a trade-dress/counterfeiting suit brought AGAINST Empower Tribe, not by it, and was dismissed after Behringer renamed its pedal.

### Inferences
- The X32/X-Air ecosystem is the closest analogue: unofficial, protocol-level third-party controllers sold commercially on the App Store for 10+ years, using a protocol Music Tribe never formally documented for X32, with Music Tribe's forum actively hosting how-to content and Music Tribe later formally authorising an outsider's protocol document for WING. This is strong evidence of tolerance-in-practice, but tolerance of one product line's community is not a licence, and Lake (touring/install market, higher-value amplifiers, Dante integration) is a different business unit with a different customer base. It is also notable that X32 OSC is a semi-open protocol (OSC is an open standard) whereas the Lake protocol may be entirely proprietary.
- Trademark: naming the app anything containing "Lake" (or "Lab.gruppen", "PLM", "Lake Controller") in the App Store title or icon would breach Apple 4.1(c)/5.2.1 and invite a Lanham Act claim; nominative/descriptive use in the description ("compatible with Lake-enabled Lab.gruppen PLM/D-Series devices; not affiliated with or endorsed by Music Tribe/Empower Tribe") is how Mixing Station and the unofficial Dante tools handle it. A trademark lawyer should sign off on the exact wording, since the EULA's IP clause says any use of Music Tribe IP "independently of the Software" is treated as infringement.
- Personal use vs. distribution: for personal use the exposure is essentially a breach-of-contract claim with nominal damages (the app is free, so "amount actually paid" is zero, and the developer's own losses are the measure only if Music Tribe sued) plus licence termination; for distribution the exposure adds (i) the EULA's "commercially exploit" clause, (ii) injunctive relief the EULA pre-agrees to, (iii) DMCA §1201(a)(2) "trafficking" theories if any circumvention were involved, (iv) trademark, and (v) an App Store takedown via Apple's IP dispute process, which is far cheaper for Music Tribe than litigation. Klon and Boss show Empower Tribe litigates and is litigated against over hardware; there is no precedent of it suing a software interoperability developer.
- Selling the app (vs. free) does not change the copyright/DMCA analysis (Connectix and Accolade were commercial competitors and still won on fair use) but increases the practical incentive for the rights-holder to act and makes the "commercially exploit" clause squarely applicable if a court reads the app as exploiting "the Software".

## Key Question 4: Dante / Audinate posture (stretch goal)

### Takeaway
Audinate's EULAs prohibit reverse engineering/decompiling its software and the Dante technology, and Audinate does not license its API to individuals, yet several unofficial Dante control implementations built from packet capture have existed publicly since 2014 (wycliffe), with an active iOS/CLI project (NetAudio) distributed via TestFlight rather than the App Store; no public Audinate legal action against them was found.

### Cited Findings
- Audinate's EULA (Dante Domain Manager) prohibits users from modifying, translating, reverse engineering, decompiling, disassembling, or creating derivative works based on the Software; Dante technology terms add that users may not "decompile, copy, reverse engineer, disassemble or otherwise reduce the Dante technology files to human-readable form" or circumvent encryption/copy protection [snippet paraphrase; page blocked] — [Audinate DDM EULA](https://www.audinate.com/legal/dante-domain-manager-end-user-license-agreement/); [Dante Ready Activator terms](https://www.audinate.com/legal/dra-terms-conditions/)
- Community report: Audinate "does not create any licenses for individuals to use their API or access the documentation on it, and you'd have to create some kind of Dante-enabled product to get access" [snippet] — [Gearspace thread: Dante routing without Dante Controller](https://gearspace.com/board/music-computers/1221989-dante-routing-without-dante-controller-possible.html)
- wycliffe (Jeff Sharkey, 2014): "Clean room implementation of Dante protocol"; states the work "doesn't appear to circumvent any protection covered under the DMCA" and "makes no claim to be either authorized or approved by Audinate Pty Ltd"; GPLv2 code (fetched) — [jsharkey/wycliffe](https://github.com/jsharkey/wycliffe)
- inferno (teodly): "Dante protocol is undocumented. Everything was reverse-engineered or based on other reverse-engineering projects"; warns "Dante uses technology patented by Audinate. This source code may use these patents too. Consult a lawyer if you want to: make money of it; distribute binaries in (or from) a region where software patents apply"; GPLv3/AGPLv3 (fetched) — [teodly/inferno](https://github.com/teodly/inferno/)
- NetAudio / network-audio-controller (Chris Ritsen): "an independent interoperability project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Audinate"; originated from "packet capture replay and modification experiments"; iOS/iPadOS app distributed via TestFlight beta only, not the App Store; Unlicense (fetched) — [chris-ritsen/network-audio-controller](https://github.com/chris-ritsen/network-audio-controller); [netaudio.app](https://netaudio.app/) (blocked); [PyPI netaudio](https://pypi.org/project/netaudio/)
- No report of Audinate legal action against any of these projects was found in searches.

### Inferences
- Dante control (routing/subscriptions) via reverse-engineered protocol is a well-trodden path with public precedent, but Audinate's patents (flagged by inferno) and tighter licensing model make Dante a higher-risk surface than Lake control, especially for a paid App Store app. Limiting the stretch goal to what Dante devices expose via standard mDNS/discovery and to read-only status, or deferring Dante routing entirely, reduces exposure.
- NetAudio's choice of TestFlight-only distribution for iOS is a signal that an unofficial Dante controller author judged App Store publication risky; that is inference from behaviour, not a stated reason.

### Gaps
- The Dante Controller EULA itself could not be fetched; only the DDM/DRA EULA language was returned in snippets. Verify whether the Dante Controller click-through licence has the same reverse-engineering clause.
- No statement of Audinate's actual stance toward unofficial controllers (tolerance or enforcement) was found in any source.

## Summary of where a lawyer's review is warranted
- Whether passive capture of your own devices' traffic breaches the "reverse engineer any part of the Software" clause under your home-state contract law (no case on point; Bowers/Davidson make contract the real US risk).
- Confirmation that the Lake protocol has no encryption/authentication that could be a §1201 "technological measure"; if it does, stop and get advice before proceeding.
- App name, icon and marketing wording versus the LAKE / LAB.GRUPPEN marks and Apple 4.1(c)/5.2.1 before any App Store submission.
- Whether the EULA's "commercially exploit ... the Software" and pre-agreed injunctive relief clauses are triggered by distributing a compatible third-party app (as opposed to distributing the Software).
- For the Dante stretch goal, Audinate patents and the Dante Controller EULA.
