# Lake's Sanctioned Protocol Stops Short of EQ

Lab.gruppen's Lake devices speak one publicly documented network protocol, Direct Lake Messaging (DLM), and every piece of evidence gathered says it reaches mutes, gains, input-router priority, preset recall, power and metering but **not EQ, delay or polarity**; those remain reachable only through Lake Controller's own undocumented UDP protocol, which nobody has publicly decoded as of September 2026. Lake Controller and DLM share the same transport family (UDP, devices addressed by a 64-bit hardware Frame ID, port 6004 on the controller side and 6015/6016 on the device side), so tomorrow's capture is well framed: you already know the header layout, class IDs and ACK codes of the *sanctioned* dialect, and the working hypothesis is that the native dialect uses the same 28-byte little-endian framing with additional packet types. The primary/secondary multi-controller feature you described is real and documented back to the 2005 Dolby-era manual, and v5.7 release notes confirm that a secondary's edits are relayed through the primary rather than written to frames directly; the approval prompt on the primary and the exact list of restricted functions are not confirmed by anything retrievable and must be observed live. Legally, passive capture on your own LAN between your own frames and software you licensed is the lowest-risk method under every regime examined, but the Empower Tribe EULA's "reverse engineer any part of the Software" clause is enforceable as a contract in the US (Bowers, Davidson v. Jung), so personal use is a nominal-damages contract exposure while App Store distribution adds trademark, "commercial exploitation" and takedown exposure that warrants counsel; EU/UK residents get a statutory, non-waivable right to observe and test. For a cross-platform controller that must serve iPad and Android and ideally run as a browser PWA, the platform answer is bridge-first: browsers have no raw UDP, so a small LAN bridge that speaks Lake UDP to the frames and WebSocket to any browser is the recommended architecture, and it also sidesteps the iOS multicast entitlement, App Store review and trademark-in-listing problems; native iPad (unicast without entitlement, broadcast only with Apple's approval and BSD sockets) and native Android (UDP freely, multicast receive with a MulticastLock) remain optional fallbacks, and none of this changes the protocol-decoding work. A crucial caveat governs the whole report: the egress proxy blocked every host carrying the primary PDFs, so unless a claim is marked as coming from a fully read source (the open-source DLM client, GitHub issues, Apple technotes, the mirrored EULA and statutes), it rests on search-result snippets of the named document.

## Evidence quality: what was read in full and what rests on snippets

The research team could not open a single Lab.gruppen, Music Tribe, distributor or manual-mirror page; the DLM v3.4 PDF, every revision of the Lake Controller Operation Manual, the PLM/PLM+/LM/D-Series manuals and all release notes were reachable only as sentence-level search excerpts. Those excerpts are verbatim or near-verbatim, so a quoted sentence is reliable, but page context, version qualifiers and the surrounding command tables are missing. Three categories of source were read completely and carry the strongest weight in this report: the TypeScript source and protocol notes of the only open-source DLM implementation, `jvhtec/streamdeck-lake-smaart`, whose author transcribed the packet layout directly from the DLM v3.4 PDF ([dlmPacket.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jvhtec/streamdeck-lake-smaart/main/com.jvhtec.lake-smaart.sdPlugin/plugin/lake/dlmPacket.ts), [lake-dlm-protocol-v3_4.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jvhtec/streamdeck-lake-smaart/main/docs/lake-dlm-protocol-v3_4.md)); Apple's networking technotes, entitlement pages and Developer Forums threads ([TN3179](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3179-understanding-local-network-privacy), [TN3151](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3151-choosing-the-right-networking-api)); and GitHub-mirrored copies of the Empower Tribe EULA, 17 U.S.C. §1201(f) and Directive 2009/24/EC ([EULA mirror](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rgon/xair-docker/6d12131a562d0fd4852b02ebdf8b010246ff5574/eula.md)). Throughout, **[full]** marks a fully read source, **[snippet]** a search excerpt of a blocked document, and **[inference]** the researchers' reasoning. Nothing about packet bytes has been reconstructed from memory; where the native protocol is concerned, the report states hypotheses to test, not facts.

## Lake control rides UDP 6004/6015 and addresses frames by hardware ID

Lake Controller's own traffic is UDP. The v5.7 release notes added "improved error messaging if UDP port 6004 is already in use by another program and the Lake Controller is unable to start" ([v5.7 release notes, snippet](https://studylib.net/doc/18304345/lake-software-release-notes)), the DLM v3.4 document warns that "Port 6004 may not be used by any other program" ([DLM v3.4, snippet](https://beetech-inc.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/DLM-Lake-3rd-party-protocol-v3_4.pdf)), and the Operation Manual states that "the Lake Controller uses UDP messaging to communicate with Lake devices on the network" ([Rev 1.5.2 manual, snippet](https://prodgsystems.com/public/pdf/128_Lake%20Controller%20Manual.pdf)). Since **v5.6 (2010)** controller-to-frame traffic moved "from UDP Broadcast to UDP Unicast, greatly reducing network traffic," a change explicitly motivated by Wi-Fi and "multiple controller applications" ([Fast-and-Wide, snippet](https://www.fast-and-wide.com/equipment-releases/amplification/722-lake-controller-v56-software)). The DLM specification, which the v8.0.0 release notes tie to the shipping firmware ("compatible with the functionality of the DLM protocol v3.4"), uses "UDP Port 6004 as the application listening port for receiving packets and UDP Port 6015 as the device destination port" ([DLM v2.8 mirror, snippet](https://docplayer.net/57698965-Direct-lake-messaging-3-rd-party-protocol-for-plm-tm-lm-tm-series.html); [v8.0.0 release notes, snippet](https://beetech-inc.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/Lake_Controller_v8-Release-Notes.pdf)). The open-source client adds a third port from the PDF: **6016 is the "dynamic" device port whose replies return to the sender's ephemeral port**, which is what lets a third-party host coexist with a running Lake Controller that owns 6004 ([lake-dlm-protocol-v3_4.md, full](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jvhtec/streamdeck-lake-smaart/main/docs/lake-dlm-protocol-v3_4.md)). The [inference] that Lake Controller itself sends to 6015 and receives on 6004 is therefore the first hypothesis to check in the capture; the manuals only ever name 6004.

Addressing is by a fixed device identity, not by IP. "If a specific unit is to be addressed the units hardware ID is used as destination ID in packet header," the ID being the front-panel Frame ID ("MENU->Frame"), and a host can instead "set the Destination Class ID to broadcast" to reach all processors ([DLM v3.4, snippet](https://beetech-inc.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/DLM-Lake-3rd-party-protocol-v3_4.pdf)). The LM Series manual confirms the Frame ID is "a unique Lake product identifier that cannot be changed" ([LM Series manual p.42, snippet](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/814603/Lake-Lake-Lm-Series.html?page=42)). The fully read DLM implementation shows the ID as a 64-bit value written `3d000011:d6ed9201` ([jvhtec README, full](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jvhtec/streamdeck-lake-smaart/main/README.md)). Discovery is zero-configuration: both PC and frames default to 169.254.x.x link-local, frames can be Auto IP, DHCP or Fixed "only via the Lake Controller software," and the manual never asks the user to type a frame address ([Network chapter, snippet](https://manuals.plus/m/8e3b70f4847d495aa867fed41e056954178270e4e526cb292b1e535cccfb686e); [LM Series manual p.42, snippet](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/814603/Lake-Lake-Lm-Series.html?page=42)). Frames announce themselves: a v5.7 note says "PLM frames with firmware 2.58 broadcast proprietary control protocol messages" ([snippet](https://beetech-inc.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/DLM-Lake-3rd-party-protocol-v3_4.pdf)), and the DLM client discovers frames by parsing broadcast announcement packets (types 4 and 5) that carry a product code, after sending its own heartbeat ([dlmPacket.ts, full](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jvhtec/streamdeck-lake-smaart/main/com.jvhtec.lake-smaart.sdPlugin/plugin/lake/dlmPacket.ts)). Expect, then, to see frame-originated broadcasts even with no controller running, plus a controller broadcast at launch, followed by unicast exchanges [inference].

Operationally, Lake Controller binds to one NIC: with two or more enabled interfaces it shows a "Network Selection Dialog" at startup ([v8.0.0 release notes, snippet](https://beetech-inc.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/Lake_Controller_v8-Release-Notes.pdf)), so the capture must run on that adapter. On PLM+ and D Series the Lake control path and Dante share the same Ethernet port and 169.254 subnet (the firmware updater "must be on the same subnet as Dante (169.254.x.x)"), which is why the manual demands a managed switch to "filter Dante audio traffic from reaching the access point" when Wi-Fi control is used ([AVL KB, snippet](https://support.avlmediagroup.com/kbase/how-do-i-update-the-firmware-in-my-lake-lm-processor-or-lab-gruppen-plm-d-series-amplifier/); [Rev 1.5.4 manual, snippet](https://www.eviaudio.fr/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Manuel-Lake-Controller.pdf)). Where state lives matters for your app's design: the frame holds the running configuration (front-panel preset recall works without a controller, and controller loss is merely a "CTRL OFFLINE" warning), while the controller holds the system view and reconciles by flagging frames "Out of Sync" and pushing via **FastSync or SlowSync**, mechanisms named only in the v8 known-issues list ([Rev 1.7.0 manual, snippet](https://manuals.plus/m/0633d4360a16681b3b477d2109fa5992f82872ddf78fd6d582cdef3aef67bed5); [v8.0.0 release notes, snippet](https://beetech-inc.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/Lake_Controller_v8-Release-Notes.pdf)). No multicast, TCP control channel, encryption or authentication handshake is mentioned anywhere retrievable; the manual's one reference to "TCP/IP networking" is generic firewall language [snippet]. Lake Controller also acts as a TCP *client* of Smaart's API and of VNC servers, neither of which is a Lake control API ([ProSoundWeb, snippet](https://www.prosoundweb.com/lake-introduces-smaart-7-integration-with-lake-controller-version-6/); [Lake Controller on the iPad, snippet](https://prodgsystems.com/public/pdf/128_Lake%20Controller%20on%20the%20iPad.pdf)).

## Primary and secondary controllers exist, but the approval flow is unverified

Your description of the arrangement is consistent with the record but only partly confirmed. **Verified [snippet]:** the current Operation Manual (Rev 1.5.4) has a "Multiple Controllers" section "with information on Primary and Secondary Controllers and Restricted Functionality" ([Rev 1.5.4 manual](https://www.eviaudio.fr/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Manuel-Lake-Controller.pdf)), and the same section headings appear in the July 2005 v3.2 User Mode Manual, so the feature predates Lab.gruppen's 2009 acquisition of Lake from Dolby ([v3.2 manual](https://manualzz.com/doc/7199931/v3.2-lake-controller-user-mode-manual)). Release history treats it as a live feature area: v5.3 (2009) brought "many improvements in the area of Super Modules and Multiple Controllers" ([LightSoundJournal](https://www.lightsoundjournal.com/2009/06/20/lake-releases-new-lake-controller-software-version-5-3/)), v5.6's unicast change targeted "multiple controller applications," and v5.7 both fixed ("Multiple Controllers are now working again") and caveated the mechanism ([v5.7 release notes](https://studylib.net/doc/18304345/lake-software-release-notes)). The most informative sentence is a v5.7 known issue: "If connection to primary Controller is lost from secondary Controller, Events and Control popup values can still be edited from secondary Controller – however these changes never reach the frames and when connection is established to primary Controller again all changes done on secondary will be lost" ([v5.7 release notes, snippet](https://studylib.net/doc/18304345/lake-software-release-notes)). This is strong evidence [inference] that **a secondary does not write to frames; its edits are relayed through the primary**, that the primary holds the authoritative controller-side model, and that enforcement is at least partly controller-side. A second v5.7 issue, "a system with more than one secondary Lake Controller has communication problems," shows the topology is one primary plus (nominally) many secondaries.

**Not verified by any source:** that the primary is prompted to approve a joining secondary, which specific functions "Restricted Functionality" removes, what happens when the primary disconnects (promotion, timeout or manual take-over), and whether frames themselves refuse writes from a non-primary host. One device-side hint exists: the DLM ACK codes transcribed from the v3.4 PDF include **ACK_NOTMASTER (-3)** alongside success, invalid-packet, DSP-error and bad-parameter codes ([dlmPacket.ts, full](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jvhtec/streamdeck-lake-smaart/main/com.jvhtec.lake-smaart.sdPlugin/plugin/lake/dlmPacket.ts)), which implies the frame has some notion of a controlling master and can reject a writer that is not it [inference]. Whether that code is returned to a second Lake Controller, to a DLM host while Lake Controller is online, or only in some other condition is unknown and is a specific thing to provoke tomorrow. The 2014 third-party iPad app "Lake Remote" advertised that it "runs seamlessly with Lake Controller, enabling multiple users to concurrently control the system in real time" ([AppAdvice, snippet](https://appadvice.com/app/lake-remote/896686441)), so concurrent control from an iPad has been done, but nothing says whether it joined as a secondary or spoke DLM directly to frames. Do not conflate this with the manual's other "Primary/Secondary" usage, which refers to redundant Dante network ports on dual-Dante products [snippet].

For your app's two modes this means: "primary when no Lake Controller is present" is the DLM-like direct-to-frame case and is the easier one; "secondary when one is" requires either speaking the controller-to-controller protocol to the primary PC (whose existence is inferred from v5.7 but whose format is entirely unknown) or writing directly to frames and accepting that Lake Controller may flag frames Out of Sync or that frames may answer NOTMASTER. The capture plan below is designed to discriminate between these outcomes.

## DLM reaches mutes, gains, routers and presets; nothing shows it reaches EQ, delay or polarity

DLM is a device-native API: "Direct Lake Messaging provides an Ethernet 3rd party protocol suitable for integration with third party control and monitoring applications such as AMX, Crestron or other custom control software," and "the protocol allows sending high-level commands as text strings that are parsed by the receiver" ([DLM v3.4, snippet](https://beetech-inc.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/DLM-Lake-3rd-party-protocol-v3_4.pdf)). It is "provided for all devices except MY8-LAKE" ([PLM/LM/D-Series manual p.292, snippet](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/738554/labgruppen-plm-20k44-plm-12k44-plm-20000q-plm-14000-plm-10000q-lm-44-lm-26-d-series-804l-d-series-2004l-d-series-1204l.html?page=292)). The PDF is publicly hosted by Lab.gruppen's Japanese distributor with no login and no NDA language surfaced in any search ([Beetech PDF](https://beetech-inc.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/DLM-Lake-3rd-party-protocol-v3_4.pdf)). The fully read open-source client gives the wire layout the PDF specifies: all fields little-endian; a **28-byte header** of source ID hi/lo (2×u32), destination ID hi/lo (2×u32), source class (u16), destination class (u16), total length (u16), packet type (u16) and message ID (u32); a payload padded to a 4-byte boundary; and a 4-byte footer that is either a 16-bit-LFSR-style checksum or zero. Class IDs are host = 6, device = 5, broadcast = 0; the broadcast destination ID is `0xFFFFFFFE:0xFFFFFFFD`; packet types are 2 (Ack), 4 (BroadcastID), 5 (MultiBroadcastID) and **701 (DLMMsg, the text carrier)**; maximum packet size is 560 bytes; and ordinary commands are sent with a zero footer while heartbeat packets carry the checksum ([dlmPacket.ts, full](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jvhtec/streamdeck-lake-smaart/main/com.jvhtec.lake-smaart.sdPlugin/plugin/lake/dlmPacket.ts)). The product codes seen in announcements span legacy PLM (0x04 10000Q, 0x06 14000, 0x09 20000Q), LM 26/44 in Contour and Mesa modes (0x0A–0x0D), PLM+ 12K44/20K44 (0x13, 0x14) and D Series 8K44/12K44/20K44 (0x15–0x17), which covers every family you named [full]. Command grammar is `Object.Property.Op Identifier [Value]` with `?` query, `=` set and `!` execute, e.g. `Mod.In.Mute=A 1`, and the PDF documents a `#n` power-channel shorthand that "works for all Mod.In and Mod.Out parameters" ([dlmCommands.ts, full](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jvhtec/streamdeck-lake-smaart/main/com.jvhtec.lake-smaart.sdPlugin/plugin/lake/dlmCommands.ts); [DLM v3.4, snippet](https://beetech-inc.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/DLM-Lake-3rd-party-protocol-v3_4.pdf)). An AMX programmer who implemented it independently called it "pretty difficult" and needed Wireshark, describing feedback as arriving on a separate port ([HARMAN AMX forum, snippet](https://proforums.harman.com/amx/discussion/8715/lab-gruppen-lake-processor-amp-control)).

Against your target parameter list, the evidence sorts as follows.

| Your requirement | DLM status | Evidence grade |
|---|---|---|
| Input mute | `Mod.In.Mute?/=` confirmed | PDF snippet + working client [full] |
| Output mute | `Mod.Out.Mute?/=` (module, channel) | Working client [full] |
| Input level | `Mod.In.Gain?/=` (dB, 2 decimals; format "assumed" by author) | Working client [full], not hardware-verified |
| Output level | `Mod.Out.Gain` listed only in the repo's web-derived notes | Plausible, unverified |
| Input/output delay | No command found in any source | Absence of evidence |

| EQ (PEQ/GEQ/Mesa) | No command found; targeted searches for Delay/Polarity/PEQ/Mesa tokens in the indexed PDF returned nothing | Absence of evidence |

The conclusion that EQ, delay and polarity are **not** in DLM is an absence-of-evidence finding, not a documented exclusion, but it is corroborated from four independent directions: the Crestron module (2009) exposes "fault reporting, muting, and soloing at the per-channel level, as well as power on/off and real time metering" ([ProSoundWeb, snippet](https://www.prosoundweb.com/lab-gruppen-joins-open-platform-crestron-integrated-partner-program-ipp/)); Beetech's native Q-SYS plugin (2019) controls "Power (On/Standby), Input Router/Mixer, and Preset Store/Recall" and monitors frame, LoadPilot and outputs ([Beetech, snippet](https://beetech-inc.com/products/nativeq-syspluginfor-dseries/)); the Companion request (2022) sought only power, preset recall and module mutes ([issue #710, full](https://github.com/bitfocus/companion-module-requests/issues/710)); and the 2026 Stream Deck plugin ships gain, mute, router priority and preset recall ([README, full](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jvhtec/streamdeck-lake-smaart/main/README.md)). By contrast the LM 44 datasheet reserves "gain, delay, limiters, EQ, crossovers and all I/O configuration and routing" to Lake Controller ([LM 44 datasheet, snippet](https://www.prosoundweb.com/images/products/113-326_prod_pdf.pdf)). One search snippet attributed a datasheet-style sentence about "Parametric EQ, shelving and all-pass filters with features for Delay, Mute, Phase, and Gain" to the DLM PDF, but the same result set included LM 44 retail copy and it cannot be treated as a command list. The only way to settle the question is the PDF's own command table, which is why that download heads tomorrow's list. Practical implication: **DLM alone delivers roughly half of your feature set (mutes, input level, router priority, presets, meters) with zero reverse engineering**, and the native protocol is needed for delay, polarity, EQ and probably output level. The robustness note that "sending invalid third-party messages (DLM) can lead to a device reset" was a *fixed* v8 issue ([v8.0.0 release notes, snippet](https://beetech-inc.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/Lake_Controller_v8-Release-Notes.pdf)); on older firmware, fuzz with care.

## Nobody has publicly decoded the native Lake protocol, while Dante control is a solved problem

GitHub code search for "Direct Lake Messaging" returns exactly two files, both in `jvhtec/streamdeck-lake-smaart`; searches for Lake Controller with wireshark, pcap, dissector or reverse-engineer terms return nothing; and no pcap, Lua dissector, library or write-up of the native controller-to-frame protocol exists anywhere the team could find ([GitHub search, performed 2026-09-29](https://github.com/jvhtec/streamdeck-lake-smaart)). That repository, active January to July 2026, has no license file, so treat its code as all-rights-reserved and re-implement from the spec rather than copy; its `docs/lake-controller-api.md` also contains an older, web-derived 12-byte header description that the author's own later PDF transcription contradicts, so the 28-byte layout is authoritative ([LICENSE 404](https://github.com/jvhtec/streamdeck-lake-smaart/blob/main/LICENSE); [lake-controller-api.md, full](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jvhtec/streamdeck-lake-smaart/main/docs/lake-controller-api.md)). Beyond it, the Lake ecosystem's third-party history is thin: the withdrawn 2014 Lake Remote iPad app, the closed Q-SYS plugin, a Crestron LM26 module ([Crestron Application Market, snippet](https://applicationmarket.crestron.com/lab-gruppen-lm26/)), integrator-written AMX code, and Lake's own VNC-to-Windows recipe for iPad ([Sound Forums, snippet](https://soundforums.net/news/lake-controller-on-the-ipad-190578/)). A native iPad Lake controller would be novel. The most useful [inference] for tomorrow is structural: DLM's `701` packet type sits in a much larger type space, and frames emit the same broadcast announcements whether or not a DLM host is present, so the native protocol most plausibly shares the ID/class/length/type/msgId framing with different type numbers and binary rather than text payloads. If the first captured Lake Controller datagram parses cleanly with the DLM header, the decode campaign starts with a working dissector skeleton on day one.

Dante control is in a different state entirely. **NetAudio** (`chris-ritsen/network-audio-controller`) is an Unlicense-licensed Python and Rust project, with a native iPhone/iPad client on TestFlight, that covers discovery, routing, names, sample rate, encoding, latency, gain, metering and Dante Domain Manager networks; it was "created by analyzing network requests and preset files made by Dante Controller," and issues were being filed as recently as 27 September 2026 ([repo](https://github.com/chris-ritsen/network-audio-controller); [issue #63](https://github.com/chris-ritsen/network-audio-controller/issues/63)). Its wiki documents the mDNS service types (`_netaudio-arc._udp`, `_netaudio-cmc._udp`, `_netaudio-chan._udp`), ports 4440/4455 for routing, 8800 for control and monitoring, 8700 for device settings, and a packet format beginning `0x27` with sequence and request IDs ([Technical details wiki](https://github.com/chris-ritsen/network-audio-controller/wiki/Technical-details)). **Inferno** implements the Dante audio transport itself in Rust under GPL/AGPL and carries an explicit patent warning ([teodly/inferno](https://github.com/teodly/inferno/)). For your input-routing stretch goal, NetAudio's Rust core is the obvious dependency, with two cautions: its parsers break on non-Brooklyn vendor implementations (Biamp, Shure), which should not affect Lake's Brooklyn-family cards, and DDM-enrolled venues deliberately block unmanaged control ([DDM guide, snippet](https://dev.audinate.com/GA/ddm/userguide/webhelp/content/enrolling_devices_in_domains.htm)). How Lake Controller itself performs Dante subscriptions (Audinate's protocol directly, or via Lake messages to the firmware) is undocumented; the v8 fix for "mismatching Dante Inputs during System File Recall" shows the controller writes subscriptions on recall, and the manual's separate "firewall access for Dante functionality" hints at extra sockets, so a capture filter limited to 6004/6015 could miss it [inference].

## Passive capture is the lowest-risk method, but a US contract claim cannot be excluded

The governing document is the shared Music Tribe brand EULA, now issued by **Empower Tribe HQ FZE** (copyright 2025), which you accept when you download Lake Controller. Read in full from a mirrored copy, it prohibits users from doing anything to "disassemble, decrypt, reverse compile, or reverse engineer any part of the Software," forbids making the Software available to third parties or commercially exploiting it, pre-agrees that breach causes "irreparable harm" justifying injunctive relief, terminates automatically on breach, and is governed by "the Laws of the jurisdiction where you are a resident"; it never defines "Software," never mentions firmware, hardware or protocols, and has no arbitration, venue or audit clause ([EULA mirror, full](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rgon/xair-docker/6d12131a562d0fd4852b02ebdf8b010246ff5574/eula.md)). The natural reading [inference] is that "Software" is the downloadable application, and the restriction is on reverse engineering *it*, not on observing what your amplifier emits on your own network; the counter-reading is that black-box analysis of the Software's output is "reverse engineering any part of the Software." No US case decides this on a passive-capture-only record, which is the single point most worth a lawyer's hour.

US law is unsettled at the contract layer and favourable at the copyright layer. Courts enforced anti-reverse-engineering licence terms in Bowers v. Baystate (Fed. Cir. 2003), holding that "the Copyright Act does not preempt or narrow the scope of the contract claim" ([Justia, snippet](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/320/1316/615597/)), and in Davidson v. Jung (8th Cir. 2005), where the bnetd developers analysed Battle.net traffic and lost on both contract and the DMCA §1201(f) defence because their circumvention "constitutes infringement" ([Google Scholar, snippet](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_case?case=5463280175310353497)). Both cases involved decompiling or emulating the licensor's code; Wireshark on your own LAN copies none of Lake Controller's code. Sega v. Accolade and Sony v. Connectix hold that intermediate copying to reach "unprotected functional elements" for interoperability is fair use ([Copyright Office summary, snippet](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/sony-connectix-9thcir2000.pdf)). The DMCA is engaged only if a "technological measure" is circumvented; if the Lake protocol proves unencrypted and unauthenticated, as all evidence suggests, §1201(a) is not triggered and §1201(f)'s interoperability exemption, read in full from a statutory mirror, is a fallback rather than a necessity ([§1201(f) text](https://github.com/gingerbeardman/canoe-disassembly)). If a challenge-response or key exchange appears in tomorrow's capture, stop and get advice before going further. Trade-secret law expressly excludes reverse engineering from "improper means" ([Finnegan on DTSA](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/what-you-should-know-about-the-defend-trade-secrets-act.html)). EU and UK residents are better placed: Directive 2009/24/EC Art. 5(3) grants a right "to observe, study or test the functioning of the program," Art. 6 permits decompilation for interoperability, Art. 8 voids contrary contract terms, and SAS v WPL confirmed functionality and data formats are not protected expression ([Directive mirror](https://github.com/Pitscheider/inim-prime-native/blob/d841840dfd69ddf467ec19e96fd2347a5e4fccdb/LEGAL.md); [SCL report on SAS v WPL](https://www.scl.org/2451-sas-institute-inc-v-world-programming-ltd-report-and-analysis/)). The 2025 IBM v LzLabs decision narrows the UK right to observation and excludes copying code ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/05/uk-court-rules-on-reverse-engineering-of-mainframe-software)), which passive capture respects.

**Personal use versus App Store distribution** differ mainly in incentive and remedy. For a private tool on your own rigs the realistic exposure [inference] is a breach-of-contract claim with nominal damages and licence termination. Distribution adds the EULA's "commercially exploit" clause, the pre-agreed injunction, trademark, and Apple's IP-dispute process, which is far cheaper for a rights-holder than litigation. Apple's guidelines, read in full, bar using "another developer's icon, brand, or product name in your app's icon or name, without approval" (4.1(c)) and "protected third-party material such as trademarks" (5.2.1), so the app cannot be called anything containing "Lake," "PLM" or "Lab.gruppen," although descriptive compatibility statements with a non-affiliation disclaimer are the industry norm ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). The closest precedent is encouraging: Mixing Station has sold on the App Store for a decade as an unofficial X32/X-Air/Midas controller, Music Tribe's own forums host how-to threads for it, and Music Tribe formally authorised Patrick-Gilles Maillot's independent WING protocol document, hosting it on its own CDN ([App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mixing-station/id1438352631); [WING Remote Protocols PDF on Music Tribe CDN](https://cdn.mediavalet.com/aunsw/musictribe/ZUwX-Z1Sn0mIn3n2KAt4fQ/68jQRA4CpEW9-Lx22AA6vg/Original/WING%20Remote%20Protocols%20FW%203.0.6.pdf)). No takedown against any third-party control app was found, and Empower Tribe's recent litigation (Klon, Boss) concerns hardware clones and patents, not interoperability software ([Guitar.com on Klon dismissal](https://guitar.com/news/klon-behringer-lawsuit-dismissed/)). Tolerance of one product line's community is not a licence, and Lake serves a higher-value touring and install market, so the sensible order is: build and use privately; when distribution is contemplated, have counsel review the name, the "commercially exploit" clause and the LAKE trademark registration; and consider approaching Lab.gruppen for the same kind of authorisation Maillot obtained. Dante is the riskier surface: Audinate's EULAs forbid reverse engineering and Inferno flags patents, and NetAudio's author chose TestFlight over the App Store, which reads as a risk judgement [inference]. Clean-room hygiene follows from the case law: your own devices and network only; never decompile Lake Controller or firmware images; no Lake code, strings, graphics or module files in the app; and a dated record of captures and hypotheses proving the information was "not previously readily available."

## The parameter model tells you what to sweep and what encodings to expect

A Lake Frame holds one to four processing Modules. "In Contour Mode, a maximum of two Modules are contained within each Frame; these are referred to as Module A and Module B," each configurable as a Classic crossover (Bessel, Butterworth, Linkwitz-Riley), a Linear Phase crossover, or full-bandwidth Auxiliary Outputs, while the LM 44 defaults to four Mesa EQ modules ([LM Quick Start p.16, snippet](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1884060/Lake-Lm-Series.html?page=16)). D Series frames take "up to four processing Modules containing a total of up to twelve processing Module outputs that can be routed to any of the four power output channels" ([D Series manual, snippet](https://www.fullcompass.com/common/files/46498-LabGruppenDSeriesOperationManual.pdf)); PLM+ is the same platform generation but its per-frame module count was not confirmed. Lake Controller v8 added **XP modules** for PLM+ and D Series with FIR-capable crossovers, "Pre-Output EQ" and up to three-band multiband limiters ([v8.0.0 release notes, snippet](https://beetech-inc.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/Lake_Controller_v8-Release-Notes.pdf)), with a DSP budget of 384 FIR taps per block and 20 biquads per output ([Eclipse Audio](https://eclipseaudio.com/lake-fir-filter/)). The input chain is physical inputs → Input Routers, each with "up to four input fail-over priority settings" → Module Input Mixer → module ([Rev 1.6.1 manual, snippet](https://www.eviaudio.fr/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Lake_Controller_Operation_Manual.pdf)). EQ is organised as stacked overlays, "with up to 256 EQs across multiple overlays," mixing classic parametric and shelving shapes with raised-cosine Mesa filters (asymmetric, with independently adjustable upper and lower slopes) and Ideal Graphic EQ bands that "sum flat" ([Lab.gruppen Lake Processing, snippet](https://www.labgruppen.com/en/lake-processing); [Mix on Mesa Quad EQ](https://www.mixonline.com/news/lake-technology-lake-mesa-quad-eq-421218)). "The maximum available user delay for Lake Contour modules is 2 seconds from any input to any output," displayable in ms, feet or metres ([svconline, snippet](https://www.svconline.com/news/lake-technology-lake-contour-365806)). Polarity buttons sit beside the mutes "for input and output channels in Designer Mode, while User Mode provides polarity of input channels only," so **switch to Designer Mode before the polarity sweeps** ([Rev 1.5.4 manual, snippet](https://www.eviaudio.fr/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Manuel-Lake-Controller.pdf)). Groups (up to 28 per device) "provide group levels, delays, EQ overlays and limiter offsets" ([ProSoundWeb v6.4](https://www.prosoundweb.com/lab-gruppen-launches-new-lake-controller-v6-4/); [Rev 1.6.1 manual, snippet](https://www.eviaudio.fr/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Lake_Controller_Operation_Manual.pdf)).

The few confirmed resolutions constrain the encoding hypotheses. MaxRMS attack is "selectable in 0.1 ms increments from 1mS to 500 ms," the limiter knee Corner "is adjustable in 0.1 dB increments" with 0 dB meaning hard knee, and both are "subject to defined level limits" that a Module file can restrict ([PLM manual p.39, snippet](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/638117/Lab-Gruppen-Plm-Series.html?page=39)); PLM+ ISVPL threshold spans 17.8–600 V ([PLM+ manual, snippet](https://www.huss-licht-ton.de/images/products_download/User_Manual_18113_1.pdf)). The LM 44 runs a 96 kHz, 32-bit floating-point path ([LM 44 datasheet, snippet](https://www.liveforsound.com/product/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/LAKE_LM44_DataSheet_liveforsound.pdf)) and PLM firmware runs on a TI C67x floating-point DSP ([former Lake engineer's portfolio](https://github.com/tinybrain/tinybra.in/blob/master/pages/portfolio/lake.md)). The first encoding hypothesis [inference] is therefore **little-endian IEEE-754 float**, consistent with DLM's little-endian header: a 1.0 ms delay would appear as `0x3F800000` if float ms, or `0x60` (96) if integer samples at 96 kHz; a 0.1 dB step would show as `0x3DCCCCCD` increments if float, or +1 if integer ×10. The overlay architecture argues that the device stores each group EQ layer separately (the 256-filter cap is device-side), whereas for gain and delay it is undetermined whether the wire carries a composite or a per-layer value with a group/overlay ID; the sweep order below is built to distinguish these. Ranges for module input/output gain, PEQ frequency/gain/Q, GEQ band gain and delay display resolution were not retrievable; read them off the GUI end-stops tomorrow and record them, since the app will need to clamp to the same limits. Expect Module-file loads and FIR coefficient imports to appear as large bulk transfers unlike the small parameter messages, and expect meters to be periodic device-to-controller messages rather than writes [inference].

## On the optional native iPad path, unicast is free but broadcast needs Apple's entitlement

The constraints in this section apply only if you choose to ship a native iPadOS app that talks to frames directly; the bridge-first architecture recommended in the Platform strategy section below removes all of them from the client. Apple's TN3179 states the rules in full. Outgoing unicast UDP to a local address triggers the Local Network privacy alert once, requires `NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription` in Info.plist, needs no entitlement, and if the user denies it the connection sits in `.waiting` with `unsatisfiedReason == .localNetworkDenied` until the user changes the setting, whereupon "the system automatically retries the connection" ([TN3179](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3179-understanding-local-network-privacy)). Sending *or receiving* UDP broadcast or multicast on real hardware requires **`com.apple.developer.networking.multicast`**, which "requires permission from Apple before you can use it in your app," obtained through a request form; the simulator ignores the requirement ([entitlement doc](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/entitlements/com.apple.developer.networking.multicast); [Apple News](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=0oi77447)). Anecdotally approvals took about five days for an app that "relies on UDP broadcasts for legacy functionality" ([Apple Forums 666240](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/666240)), which is your case exactly; file the request before tomorrow so device testing of discovery is not blocked. Apple DTS is blunt about API choice: Network framework's broadcast support is "so limited as to be useless in practice," so use BSD sockets with `SO_BROADCAST`, bound to a specific interface by index via `IP_BOUND_IF`, never hard-coding `en0`; multicast can use `NWConnectionGroup` ([Apple Forums 772642](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/772642)). Regressions on iOS 18 and iPadOS 26 broke `NWConnection` broadcast even with the entitlement granted ([Apple Forums 771139](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/771139); [Apple Forums 805719](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/805719)). Background execution does not exist for a UDP controller: "if the app gets suspended, there's no way to resume it based on UDP traffic," and receive "stops reliably ~30 seconds after the screen locks" ([Apple Forums 757385](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/757385); [Apple Forums 822012](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/822012)), so design for foreground-only operation with teardown and full state re-request on every foreground transition, set `UIRequiresPersistentWiFi`, and disable auto-lock on the show iPad. USB-C iPads accept USB-C Ethernet adapters that appear as a broadcast-capable local interface ([9to5Mac, snippet](https://9to5mac.com/2023/05/17/use-ethernet-with-ipad/)); pin unicast to it with `NWParameters.requiredInterfaceType = .wiredEthernet`.

These constraints map cleanly onto Lake. Once a frame's IP is known, all DLM traffic in dynamic mode (device port 6016, replies to your ephemeral port) is plain unicast and needs only the Local Network prompt; the fixed mode's requirement to own local port 6004 collides with a co-resident Lake Controller only on the *same* machine, which never applies to an iPad, but dynamic mode remains the right default. Discovery is the hard part: both DLM and (inferred) the native protocol find frames through frame-originated broadcasts, so a native first release can ship without the entitlement by letting the user enter frame IPs or sweeping the /24 with unicast probes, adding broadcast discovery once Apple approves. The X32Remote app illustrates the client architecture to copy on any platform: one connection object with a lock-protected state store, a keepalive every 10 s, meters coalesced into one blob per 500 ms and UI updates batched at 100 ms ([glaypan/X32Remote](https://github.com/glaypan/X32Remote)). Keep the codec platform-neutral so the same pcap-derived fixtures drive the bridge, any native client's unit tests and the Wireshark Lua dissector.

## Platform strategy: a LAN bridge serves every screen and dodges the entitlement

This section is general platform knowledge rather than a finding from the research notes, except where the iPadOS constraints above are referenced. The decisive fact is that **browsers have no raw UDP**. A PWA running in Safari, Chrome or Firefox cannot open a UDP socket: the Direct Sockets API is not available to ordinary web apps and does not exist in Safari, and the transports a browser does offer, WebSocket, WebRTC data channels and WebTransport, all terminate at a server or a signalled peer, never at an arbitrary UDP port on a Lake frame. A pure PWA therefore cannot talk to frames directly, cannot hear their broadcast announcements, and cannot implement either DLM or the native protocol on its own. That is not a reason to abandon the browser; it is the reason to put the protocol layer somewhere else.

The recommended architecture is a small bridge process on the amp LAN: a Raspberry Pi or similar small box, or a background service on the Lake Controller PC itself, that speaks Lake UDP (DLM now, the native dialect as it is decoded) to the frames and exposes a WebSocket API plus a single web UI to any browser. One code base then serves iPad, Android tablets, phones and laptops, and the client never touches UDP, broadcast or multicast. This removes, in one move, the obstacles the notes identified for a native iPad client: no `com.apple.developer.networking.multicast` request and no BSD-socket broadcast code, because the bridge does discovery on the wired LAN; no App Store review, because the UI is a web page; and no trademark-in-listing exposure under Apple guideline 4.1(c)/5.2.1, because nothing is listed. The bridge also holds device state and keepalives while a tablet sleeps (browsers, like iPad apps, lose network when backgrounded), can rate-limit and serialise writes from several screens so your own multi-client story does not depend on Lake's primary/secondary mechanism, and is the natural home for the pcap capture and Lua dissector during the decode campaign. Costs are one more device and power supply in the rack, one extra hop of latency, the need to find the bridge (a fixed IP, a QR code or mDNS, which a browser reaches by hostname), and securing it, since anything that can reach its WebSocket can move faders on live amplifiers; bind it to the control VLAN and require a token or pairing step. The DLM client already in the open-source Stream Deck plugin is written in TypeScript, so a Node bridge could be up against the sanctioned protocol within days, and the same bridge process can run on the Lake Controller PC on show days when a Pi is not wanted.

Native fallbacks remain available if a bridge-free app is ever wanted. Android is the easy case: ordinary apps can send and receive UDP unicast, broadcast and multicast without any special approval; receiving multicast on Wi-Fi requires acquiring a `WifiManager.MulticastLock` so the radio does not filter multicast frames, and the same holds for broadcast on some devices, but there is no entitlement, no review gate and no interface-binding requirement comparable to iOS. iPad can do unicast natively today, per the constraints in the previous section, with broadcast discovery gated on Apple's entitlement. If the web UI matures first, it can later be wrapped with Capacitor (or a similar shell) to produce installable iPad and Android apps that reuse the same HTML/JS, adding a native UDP plugin only where a bridge-free mode is required; that wrapped app would then re-inherit the iOS entitlement and App Store considerations, so treat it as a later phase.

Nothing about tomorrow's capture or the decode campaign changes with this decision. The protocol layer is the same whether it lives in a Swift app, a Kotlin app or a Node or Python process on a Pi; only its location moves. Design the codec as a platform-neutral library with pcap-derived test fixtures from the outset, so the bridge, any future native client and the Wireshark dissector are all driven from the same corpus.

## Capture session plan

Capture on the Lake Controller PC itself: it is an endpoint of every unicast flow and receives every broadcast, so no SPAN port or tap is needed unless you later want to watch a second controller PC's traffic from a third machine. Wireshark's Npcap driver sees offloaded packets differently from the wire, so disable checksum offload, LSO and RSC on the capture NIC beforehand or expect "bad checksum" flags and oversized outbound frames ([Npcap guide, full](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nmap/npcap/master/docs/npcap-guide.xml)). Use a ring buffer so a long session cannot fill the disk: `tshark -i <N> -f "udp or arp" -b filesize:51200 -b files:20 -w C:\caps\lake.pcapng` (filesize is in kB per the man page; [tshark.adoc, full](https://gitlab.com/wireshark/wireshark/-/raw/master/doc/man_pages/tshark.adoc)). Do not pre-filter to 6004/6015 on day one; you want to see whether Dante control, mDNS or unexpected ports appear. Keep a timestamped action log (a text file with one line per GUI action, written from the same PC so the clocks match; a screen recording with a burned-in clock is a useful backup), wait two seconds between actions so packet windows do not overlap, and after each block save the ring files and the log together.

1. **Setup.** Record Lake Controller version and Installer number, each frame's model, firmware and Frame ID (front panel MENU→Frame, or I/O Config in the controller), each frame's IP mode and address, and the PC's IP. Confirm which NIC Lake Controller's startup dialog selected and capture on that one. Put Lake Controller in Designer Mode so output polarity is available. Load a known Module file onto Module A (a Classic 2-way or 3-way) and, if a PLM+ or D Series frame is present, an XP module onto another so both parameter families are exercised. Note every GUI end-stop (min, max, step) as you reach it during the sweeps.
2. **Baseline idle, controller absent.** Start the capture with Lake Controller closed and observe for 60 s: frame-originated broadcasts, their period, destination (255.255.255.255 or subnet), ports and payload size (DLM announcements are 68 bytes; check whether native announcements match). Then send one DLM discovery heartbeat and one `Mod.In.Mute?A` in dynamic mode from a laptop tool (or the jvhtec mock as a reference) to obtain a labelled DLM packet for header comparison.
3. **Controller launch and idle.** Launch Lake Controller and watch discovery: what it broadcasts, how frames answer, and when traffic switches to unicast; note the port pair actually used. Leave it idle on the home screen for 60 s to learn keepalive traffic and period. Open one module's Levels window and idle another 60 s, then close it, to separate meter streams from control traffic.
4. **Connect and disconnect.** Unplug one frame's Ethernet for 30 s and replug: capture the loss detection, the front-panel CTRL OFFLINE timing, the re-discovery and any Out of Sync flag and re-sync burst. Close Lake Controller normally and reopen with Recall Last System Configuration. Change one parameter on the frame's front panel while the controller is closed, then reopen the controller to force an Out of Sync condition and observe how it resolves (FastSync versus SlowSync burst sizes).
5. **Second-controller approval flow.** Start Lake Controller on a second PC on the same segment. Record whether the first controller shows any prompt, what the second shows, and, critically, whether the second's datagrams go to the frames or to the first PC (controller-to-controller traffic on the PC network). Make one gain change from the secondary and confirm whether frames receive it from the secondary's IP or the primary's. Try a restricted action from the secondary (module file load, preset store, I/O Config) and record which are greyed out. Close the primary and watch: does the secondary become primary, do the frames show CTRL OFFLINE, and are secondary edits lost as the v5.7 note describes. Finally, with the primary online, send a DLM `Mod.In.Mute=A 1` from the laptop and look for ACK_NOTMASTER (-3) versus ACK_SUCCESS (-2) and for whether the controller's GUI updates.
6. **Mutes.** Module A input mute on, off; output channel 1 mute on, off; all-output mute if present. These are the cleanest one-bit changes and establish the write, ack and echo message shapes.
7. **Levels.** Input gain: nudge by the smallest step, then +1 dB, then to max and to min; repeat for output channel 1 gain. Compare candidate 4-byte windows as little-endian float against the GUI value; a 0.1 dB nudge that produces `0x3DCCCCCD`-sized float deltas or +1 integer deltas settles the encoding early.
8. **Polarity.** Input polarity flip and restore; output channel 1 polarity flip and restore (Designer Mode).
9. **Delay.** Input delay to 1.000 ms, 10.000 ms, then the 2 s ceiling; output channel 1 delay likewise; then switch display units ms→ft→m and re-enter the same physical value to prove units are display-only. Test the 96-sample hypothesis for 1.000 ms.
10. **EQ.** On the input PEQ overlay: add a parametric filter; move frequency; move gain by one step; change bandwidth; toggle the Q/BW display preference and re-send; change type to low shelf, high shelf and Mesa; on the Mesa filter adjust lower edge, upper edge, lower slope and upper slope separately; bypass the filter; bypass the overlay; add a second overlay; delete a filter. On the GEQ overlay move one band, then flatten all, to see whether bands travel individually or as a vector. On the output side, change one crossover frequency, one slope (6→48 dB/oct), and the type Bessel→Butterworth→Linkwitz-Riley; on the XP module change the FIR crossover and a Pre-Output EQ filter.
11. **Group versus module.** Create a group, add Module A, change group gain alone, then group delay, then add a group EQ overlay and a limiter offset; put the module in a second group and change that group's gain; remove it from a group. Compare payloads with step 7 to learn whether the wire carries per-layer values with a group ID or a controller-computed composite.
12. **Routing, limiters and frame-level operations.** Input-router priority Force 1→4→Auto (compare with the DLM `Dev.Router.ForceInputPriority` reply format); one Module Input Mixer crosspoint; output-to-amp routing (D Series or PLM); MaxRMS attack 1.0→1.1→500 ms, Corner 0→-0.1 dB, MaxPeak and MaxRMS thresholds; ISVPL threshold on PLM+; frame preset store and recall; Module file load (expect a bulk transfer); lock/password; Dante input selection and, if applicable, a Dante subscription change while also watching UDP 4440/4455/8700/8800 and mDNS 5353.
13. **Read-back and metering.** Store a System file, change three parameters, recall the file and capture the write burst; compare with a recall after a Module file change to provoke SlowSync. Then open a module window with live signal for 60 s to characterise meter messages (rate, size, which meters), followed by 60 s of a Levels window with no signal and 60 s on the home screen, so meter traffic can be subtracted from every other window in the analysis.

After the session, feed message-plus-footer pairs to CRC RevEng (`reveng -w 32 -s ...`) only if the footer proves non-zero on native packets, and build the Lua dissector incrementally from the DLM header layout, registering it on the observed port and moving bytes out of an "undecoded" field as each is understood ([WSDG Lua support, full](https://gitlab.com/wireshark/wireshark/-/raw/master/doc/wsdg_src/wsdg_lua_support.adoc); [CRC RevEng README](https://github.com/JayBrown/reveng---CRC-RevEng)). Share pcapng files and the action log as files rather than exposing rpcapd or a UDP relay from the amp LAN.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the project from "reverse-engineer Lake Controller for an iPad app" to a bridge-first, two-tier build that serves iPad, Android and the browser from one protocol layer. Tier one is a DLM client, most naturally living in the LAN bridge, that needs no capture at all and already covers mutes, input level, router priority, presets and metering on every family you own; tier two is the native protocol, needed for delay, polarity, EQ and probably output level, and the sanctioned protocol hands you its header, class IDs, ID format and ACK vocabulary as the starting point for decoding it. That inheritance is the strongest reason to expect tomorrow to be productive: the first question the capture answers, within minutes, is whether Lake Controller's datagrams parse with the 28-byte DLM header, and if they do, the campaign is a matter of enumerating packet types and value encodings rather than discovering a format from scratch. The second question, whether frames answer NOTMASTER to a non-primary writer, decides whether "secondary mode" is a protocol problem or a UX problem.

Two findings should shape decisions beyond the capture. The multi-controller mechanism is controller-relayed rather than frame-arbitrated as far as the evidence goes, which means a faithful "secondary" implementation depends on an undocumented controller-to-controller dialogue that no third party has ever needed, whereas a direct-to-frame app with clear Out of Sync handling is achievable and matches what the 2014 Lake Remote app apparently did. And the legal centre of gravity is contract, not copyright: passive capture on your own LAN is defensible everywhere and statutorily protected in the EU/UK, and a bridge-served web UI avoids the store-listing questions altogether, but the moment a native app is named, sold or submitted, the questions of trademark, "commercial exploitation" and Apple's dispute process arrive together, and the X32 and WING precedents suggest that asking Lab.gruppen for the same authorisation Music Tribe gave the WING community may be cheaper than defending against it.
